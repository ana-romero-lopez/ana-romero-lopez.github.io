01
Competitive intelligence · Product committee · IBERONEX
Role: Research design, fieldwork and analysis

Mystery shopping of 37 competitors to shape the 2027 programme launch

Challenge

Before launching its 2027 programme portfolio, the product committee needed evidence on how francophone institutions active in Africa attract and convert prospects, and how IBERONEX compares.

Method

  • 37 institutions across 7 programme areas: 31 from the committee's list and 6 identified through Google, ChatGPT, paid search and the press.
  • One week of fieldwork with a standardised prospect profile across web forms, email and WhatsApp, using scripted objections (price, workload, accreditation) and a fixed follow-up protocol.
  • A 0–100 scoring model in two halves: commercial (response time, quality, follow-up, omnichannel) and digital marketing (landing page, SEO and visibility in Google and ChatGPT, Meta Ads, social media, Google Maps).

Key findings

  • Reactive sales processes: 13 of 37 institutions never replied and none made an outbound call.
  • WhatsApp as the main channel: 47% of contacts happened there, and a second email recovered replies from 6 of 12 non-responsive institutions.
  • A shared pattern among the top five: a time-limited incentive, a brochure sent on request and a complete landing page with a direct contact channel.
  • A pricing gap: local certificates and European programmes sit at opposite ends of the price range, leaving the middle band largely uncovered.
  • Low ad differentiation: only 10 of 37 run Meta ads, and few show price, testimonials or European accreditation.

From the 31-slide committee report (in Spanish): scoring model and contact funnel. Competitor names removed.

Outcome

Among the 36 institutions scored on digital marketing, IBERONEX ranked 2nd. The report led to three recommendations for the committee: time-limited incentives, informative creatives (format, duration, start date, partner logo) and a faster mobile landing page featuring African alumni testimonials.

02
Full-funnel analytics · Marketing ops · IBERONEX
Role: Designed and built it

A full-funnel dashboard connecting marketing, sales and CRM by programme, intake and country

Challenge

Campaign, sales and CRM data were reported separately: Meta lead reports for Africa and LATAM, agency paid and organic reports, opportunities in the Atenea CRM (Dynamics 365), the sales log and enrolment targets per intake. Marketing reported leads, sales reported enrolments, and there was no shared view of which campaigns actually produced students.

What it covers · 11 views in 4 areas

  • Strategy: overview and a marketing-to-sales view with lead-to-enrolment conversion by campaign, programme, intake and country. Each campaign is classified automatically as Scale · Converts despite friction · Volume without quality · Review.
  • Digital marketing: lead quality by campaign, country and programme, weekly lead evolution, and agency paid and organic KPIs by objective and geographic focus.
  • Sales: the opportunity pipeline from Atenea, time to first contact and bottlenecks per advisor, unreachable leads, loss reasons, and enrolments against target for each intake.
  • Data governance: a validation layer that checks every upload before it reaches reporting — country-code mapping, campaign-name consistency between Meta and the CRM, out-of-range engagement values and reporting-period alignment. Exceptions are flagged for review and source data is never overwritten.

How it is built

A single-file web app (HTML, JavaScript, SheetJS, Chart.js) that reads the original Excel exports directly, with no server or licence. It applies the team's business rules — Wednesday-to-Tuesday commercial weeks, campaign-name mapping across file versions, pacing against each intake's start date — and is refreshed with every CRM and Meta export.

Lifecycle & CRM · IBERONEX
Role: Flow design, copy and implementation with Sales

Lifecycle flows to convert, re-engage and retain leads and graduates

Challenge

Paid media generated volume, but a significant share of leads was never reached by sales, past students were not being re-engaged, and programmes lacked local social proof.

Three flows

  • Stage-based lead nurturing (Dynamics 365): email templates by pipeline stage, handed from Marketing to Sales for Africa and LATAM, in French and Spanish.
  • Reactivation of unreachable leads: follow-up emails and WhatsApp surveys to recover contacts sales could not reach.
  • Graduate cross-sell journey: a three-phase route offering a leadership pathway to past students — landing page, advisor training, two emails, a call campaign and a time-limited offer.

Testimonials system

A repeatable process to collect alumni and faculty testimonials: a tracking sheet fed from the CRM, an email flow triggered three to four weeks after each intake starts, a short interview guide and video specifications.

06
Marketing automation · AI · Otomatico & IBERONEX · Open source
Role: Designed and built it at Otomatico, now also used at IBERONEX · Python, Meta Marketing API, Claude API

Meta Ads AI Monitor: daily campaign diagnosis with the Claude API

Challenge

Reviewing CPL, CTR, CPM and frequency campaign by campaign is time-consuming, and performance drops are often detected only after the budget has been spent. Turning the numbers into the right action also requires context.

What I built

  • Daily pipeline at 6:00: pulls every campaign from the Meta Marketing API and compares the last 7 days with the previous 7.
  • Threshold alerts on CTR, CPL, CPM and frequency, with CPL calculated on lead actions only.
  • AI differential diagnosis (Claude API): the prompt requires the model to cross at least two metrics and assign one of nine root causes, such as creative fatigue, audience saturation, delivery restriction or a post-click funnel issue.
  • Actionable output: for each campaign, a priority level, a specific action, its rationale and one common mistake to avoid.
  • HTML email report with colour-coded week-on-week changes.

What Drives Me

Beyond the campaign

Beyond campaign performance, I care about what digital media is built for and who it includes. The platforms we choose, the content we create and the audiences we target carry a responsibility to reflect the full diversity of human experience.

That is why I research neurodivergence and AI: how artificial intelligence can be used to design more inclusive digital environments rather than replicate neurotypical norms.

I want to contribute to a digital landscape where inclusion is part of the strategy from the start, not an afterthought.

What Drives Me

Beyond the campaign

Beyond building campaigns that perform, I care deeply about what digital media is built for and who it includes. I believe that the way we design digital experiences, the platforms we choose, the content we create, and the audiences we target, carries a responsibility to reflect and serve the full diversity of human experience.

That conviction drives my interest in neurodivergence and AI, specifically in how artificial intelligence can be used not to replicate neurotypical norms, but to design more inclusive, more equitable digital environments. If the tools we build only work for some people, they are not truly working.

I want to contribute to a digital landscape where inclusion is built into the strategy from the start, not added as an afterthought. That means designing campaigns with cognitive diversity in mind, asking who is being left out, and using data and technology to build better answers.

Research · Theoretical Thesis
Multisensory Perception, Neurodivergence and Artificial Intelligence

A theoretical hypothesis exploring how different perceptual profiles, including neurodivergent and neurotypical, receive the same idea differently when it is presented in different sensory formats generated through AI.

The study builds a conceptual framework for understanding how AI-generated stimuli interact with divergent perceptual systems, identifying the conditions, paradigms, and design decisions any empirical study in this field will need to resolve.

Systematic evaluation of 8 stimulus candidates against 6 neuroscientific criteria
Analysis of AI training bias and its impact on neurodivergent representation
Framework for inclusive digital design informed by sensory processing research
Advocates for AI systems that serve cognitive diversity, not just neurotypical norms
